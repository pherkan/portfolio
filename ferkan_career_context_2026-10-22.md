### Case study 1 — Exact: Redefining bank automation success and creating an improvement cadence

**Status:** Confirmed/recounted; precise metrics and attribution need verification.  
**Useful for:** Product impact, outcome metrics, execution, prioritization, scaling improvement processes.

**Situation / problem**
- In a mature banking automation product, activity and matching/allocation metrics alone were not a complete representation of the customer outcome.
- The practical goal was to **reduce human/manual effort** associated with processing banking transactions.
- Improvement work historically moved relatively slowly, with roughly one improvement at a time in a previously discussed comparison.

**Ferkan's actions**
- Reframed success around how much human work the product removed, instead of simply counting matched or allocated transactions.
- Consolidated product/operational information into a recurring monthly view.
- Used impact-versus-effort thinking to identify and sequence incremental improvement opportunities.
- Drove a more active improvement cadence with engineering and partners.

**Reported result**
- Described approximately **12 incremental improvements** during his initial period in the role (roughly six to eight months in informal retellings), compared with a much slower previous pace.
- Some improvements were associated with an approximately **3–4% improvement** in automation; the exact denominator, whether relative or percentage-point change, and causal attribution **have not been established**.

**Core takeaway**
- Make the business/customer outcome measurable, then organize small, sustained changes around that outcome rather than treating feature delivery as success.

**Questions to verify before an interview:** What was the baseline automation rate? How was manual effort quantified? Which improvements actually shipped? What timeframe is comparable? Was the 3–4% change attributable to one release, a cluster of changes, or external factors?

### Case study 2 — Exact: Discovering an upstream Bank Gateway bottleneck

**Status:** Confirmed/recounted; fix/outcome timing not confirmed.  
**Useful for:** Technical ambiguity, root-cause analysis, cross-team dependencies, systems thinking, influencing without authority.

**Situation / problem**

**Core takeaway**

### Case study 6 — Exact: Safe fuzzy matching of bank transaction names

**Status:** Product refinement/specification work discussed in October 2026; **do not assume shipped**.  
**Useful for:** Detailed PM-to-engineering handoff, financial accuracy, risk-aware automation, phased rollout, ambiguous matches.

**Problem**
- Incoming bank transactions may mention a company name that differs slightly from the account/customer name stored in Exact.
- Example: transaction `Robotics` versus account `Robbotics`, `Roboticss`, or longer names such as `Robotics Station`.
- False positives can result in accounting errors; high precision matters more than generating many plausible matches.

**Recommended/refined direction from prior discussions**
1. **Single-character obvious typos:** Introduce conservative typo tolerance, especially one-character insertions/deletions after normalization; only suggest when the candidate is unambiguous. Exact allowed edit types and thresholds should be finalized with engineering.
2. **Legal entity suffixes:** Normalize suffixes such as `B.V.`, `BV`, `V.O.F.`, `VOF`, `N.V.`, `NV`, `C.V.`, `CV` when they are identifiable legal-form suffixes, rather than a distinctive part of a name.
3. **Dutch name abbreviations:** Treat abbreviated forms such as `DIRK VDBROEK` versus `Dirk van den Broek` as a **separate rule-based matching problem**, not automatically as a simple spelling typo.
4. **Conservative automation:** Avoid automatically posting/matching on weak name similarity; in the initial narrowly scoped PBI, return suggestions rather than unsafe automatic matches.
5. **Ambiguity protection:** Reject or deprioritize ambiguous candidates; normalization first; don't let an extra distinctive word (e.g. `Robotics` versus `Robotics Station`) count as a trivial edit.
6. **Measurement:** Evaluate suggestion acceptance, precision, false positives, coverage/recall, and errors on real transaction/account pairs. An extremely high automated-match precision target such as **99.9%** was raised as a **suggested design goal**, not a currently achieved KPI.

**Examples considered**

| Incoming bank text | Stored account name | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| `Robotics` | `Robbotics` | Obvious extra-character typo could qualify as a suggestion. |
| `Robotics` | `Roboticss` | Obvious extra-character typo could qualify as a suggestion. |
| `Robotics` | `Robotics Station` | Different distinctive token; do not automatically infer identity. |
| `Mediamarkt` | `Media Markt` | Whitespace/normalization scenario. |
| `DIRK VDBROEK` | `Dirk van den Broek` | Dutch prefix/abbreviation case; separate rule. |
| `The Body Company` | `The Company` | Missing distinctive word; not an obvious typo. |

**Result**
- Prior conversations shaped this into **three separate PBIs/refinement topics**, not a proven production implementation.

### Case study 7 — Exact: Transaction-suggestion retention and database growth

**Status:** Product/data-scaling research; decision/result not confirmed.  
**Useful for:** System scaling, data lifecycle, storage cost versus user experience, setting product requirements with engineering.

**Situation / problem**
- The bank automation system may store approximately **three general-ledger suggestions per bank transaction**.
- Existing retention being discussed was around **90 days**, whereas bookkeeping users may review or process relevant transactions **seven to nine months** later.
- Extending all suggestion records indiscriminately risked increasing database size by approximately **3–4×** (rough planning estimate).

**Product trade-off**
- Preserve suggestions long enough to help real delayed review workflows while avoiding disproportionate storage/compute burden.
- Explore **selective retention** instead of simply keeping all candidates for everyone.

**Open questions**
- Which suggestions genuinely need retention? For which user journeys and accounting states? What happens when suggestions expire? What is regenerated on demand? What is the actual storage/cost profile?

**Result**
- Research and option exploration were discussed; **no confirmed shipped retention policy or data reduction** should be claimed.
